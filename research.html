PRINZ & RAINZ CDT

RAINZ CDT doctoral training started September 2026 (Year 1 at Manchester). PhD project at the University of Glasgow from September 2027.

My PhD project, Privacy-Preserving Robotic Inspection of Net-Zero Infrastructure (PRINZ), will explore how inspection robots can monitor infrastructure while minimising privacy risks for workers and nearby communities. Supervisors: Professor Mohamed Khamis and Dr Euan McGookin.

RAINZ is the EPSRC Centre for Doctoral Training in Robotics and AI for Net Zero, bringing together the universities of Manchester, Glasgow and Oxford. Its four-year programme combines robotics and AI training with research supporting the management of critical infrastructure.

The programme follows a first year of taught MSc-level training at Manchester, followed by three years focused on the doctoral project at the host institution.

WordMate: recognising toys

MSc Applied Artificial Intelligence and User Experience · Abertay University · 2026.

I developed and evaluated an object-perception component for WordMate, an educational social-robotics project. The study asked whether a detector could recognise a domain-specific toy vocabulary, which classes were least reliable, and whether inference was fast enough for interactive use.

What I did

Assembled 1,176 RGB images using GoPro HERO12 Black and Intel RealSense D435i cameras, manually annotated objects, reviewed labels with a model-assisted audit, and consolidated the taxonomy to 43 classes. Fine-tuned COCO-pretrained YOLO11s at 640 × 640 input resolution using five-fold multi-label-stratified cross-validation.

What I found

Across held-out folds, mean mAP@50 was 0.901 (SD 0.011), mAP@50:95 was 0.641, precision was 0.897 and recall was 0.851. Correcting training annotations increased recall from 0.780 to 0.846 on the same corrected validation reference.

Track and bridge pieces remained difficult, particularly within cluttered constructions. Reported inference throughput was 310.5 FPS on the tested GPU and 24.7 FPS on CPU.

What this means

The results support component-level feasibility within the sampled conditions. They do not establish generalisation to every classroom or educational benefit. Next steps include annotated live-camera evaluation on robot hardware and targeted collection of occluded construction pieces.

Supervisors: Dr Marwan Mohammed and Professor Vera Kempe.

Curriculum learning via multi-armed bandits

CMP511 Artificial Intelligence and Machine Learning · Abertay University · Submitted May 2026.

Can a teacher algorithm decide what a small language model should practise next? Arithmetic problems such as Compute 7+3−2= provided a controlled setting: difficulty was set by the number of operations (one to six), and inputs varied in range and sign to test generalisation.

What I did

Built a teacher–student framework in which a bandit teacher (UCB, discounted UCB or Thompson sampling) chose training tasks for DistilGPT-2 or GPT-2, rewarded by drops in validation error. I compared these against uniform sampling and a fixed easy-to-hard curriculum under an equal training budget, with three seeds per method, plus reward and model-size ablations.

What I found

The teacher learned a structured curriculum and shaped early training, but did not consistently outperform uniform sampling, which gave the highest and most stable accuracy on the easiest problems. The fixed easy-to-hard curriculum performed worst. On harder and distribution-shifted problems, every method stayed close to zero accuracy. A larger model helped slightly on simple tasks only.

What this means

In this setting, curriculum learning changed optimisation behaviour more than final capability. Noisy, shifting reward signals, a mismatch between reward and goal, and limited model capacity all constrained it.

Forgetting of conjunctive versus relational bindings

BSc Psychology dissertation · University of Edinburgh · 2025.

Compared forgetting of integrated shape–colour combinations with associations between distinct features. The dissertation reports an analysed sample of 82 participants. All completed immediate testing; separate groups returned after one or seven days. Analyses compared recall and examined associations with self-reported memory.

There was no significant difference in forgetting rates between binding types by seven days. The conjunctive group performed better at immediate recall, while subjective memory scores did not correlate with objective performance.

Analyses were conducted in R, comparing absolute and relative memory scores. The report also checked the influence of reminiscence and immediate-recall outliers. A key methodological takeaway is to distinguish initial recall performance from subsequent forgetting, rather than assuming a stronger starting score means slower loss. This connects my psychology background with my interest in perception.

Study undertaken with Vinaya Ganapathi; supervised by Professor Sergio Della Sala and Mario Cecchini.

Handedness & precision grasping

Third-year group research · University of Edinburgh · 2023–2024.

Sixty adults completed LEGO-style pattern-building tasks under bimanual and unimanual conditions. The study compared spontaneous hand preference with performance asymmetry.

Both handedness groups favoured their dominant hand. Greater performance asymmetry was associated with stronger grasping bias. The study did not support a general right-hand preference in visually guided precision grasping.

Group contributors: Leyla Yanik, Georgia Swift, Eve Wane-Smith, Maya Dandy-Hughes, Charlotte Williams, Clara Cheng, Yupu Hao and Abbi Smyth. Supervisor: Professor Robert McIntosh. I wrote an individual mini-dissertation on the group’s data.
